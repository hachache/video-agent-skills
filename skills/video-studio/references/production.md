# Production choices that improve the film

Start with the subject and audience. Record the single takeaway, the opening
promise, the evidence, visual language, format and intended ending in the project.
Infer ordinary choices when the creator delegates them. Continue work already
approved in the conversation rather than adding fresh confirmation stages.

## Compose before decorating

- Choose one visual protagonist per shot. Decide what the viewer reads first,
  what changes, and what has become clear when the shot ends.
- Use the creator's fonts, palette and actual material. A tuned library shot
  contributes choreography; adapt its styling and copy to this subject.
- For a Shotcraft card, look up its exact name in `gallery/api/library.json`, read
  the card's known pitfalls and open the referenced TSX. Copy its required fixtures
  and textures. Its demo source contains tuned timing that a loose imitation loses.
- For a HyperFrames effect, search the registry before inventing a replacement.
  Keep installed blocks editable. Follow its composition contract, not the
  short-editing engine's `window.setT` contract; render a finished clip to move
  between engines.
- Use layers, a camera move or 3D to explain a relationship or present a real
  object. Motion density and the number of installed effects are not quality goals.

## Put picture and sound on one timeline

Use the actual word timings for narration. Use a verified beat grid for
music-driven sequences. Share important event times between the animation and
its sound cues. Music can support sentence rhythm without dictating every cut.

Keep breath and readable holds where they serve the message. A lead phrase can
arrive quickly while its explanation stays long enough to understand. Use a small
number of significant sound accents. Existing voice, images and music come first;
new generation or asset purchasing follows the request and available tools.

## Check what the audience receives

1. Read the first frame and the first second at phone size. The promise or subject
   must already be understandable.
2. Render one representative shot before expanding the visual treatment. Compare
   its type, layout, movement and texture with the selected reference.
3. Check scene ends and transition neighbors, not only attractive middle frames.
   Seek to the same frame after earlier and later frames to expose stateful motion.
4. Check captions, face visibility, mobile safe areas, source labels, cropped text
   and numbers. Recompose portrait and landscape layouts instead of letterboxing.
5. Inspect the encoded output's dimensions, frame rate, duration and decode
   health. When audio exists, check its duration and loudness, then review sync.
6. Use contact sheets and targeted short renders for revision. A quiet hold is not
   automatically a defect. Frame-specific comments should refer to the exact draft.

Respect the selected skill's preview/export workflow and the creator's requested
deliverable. Say which aspects were actually checked; viewing a contact sheet does
not establish that somebody listened to the sound.
